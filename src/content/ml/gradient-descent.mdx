## Introduction

Gradient Descent is an optimization method used to **reduce the value of a function**. Imagine standing on a hill with your eyes closed: the slope beneath your feet tells you which direction leads downhill.

Instead of guessing where the minimum lies, the algorithm uses that local slope to decide its next move. Repeating the process produces a sequence of points that may approach a minimum.

## The Mathematics

For a differentiable one-dimensional function, we update our current point using:

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n - \alpha f'(x_n)
$$

Here, $\alpha$ is the **learning rate** (step size) and $f'(x_n)$ is the **gradient** at the current point.

A positive gradient means the curve rises as you move right, so the update moves left. A negative gradient moves you right. The learning rate controls how far you travel on each step.

### 01 — Choose a function

Start with `x^2`, which has a minimum at zero. Try **Double well** or **Wave** from the presets to explore more interesting shapes.

A small learning rate takes cautious steps. A large one can move faster, but it may jump past the minimum or even diverge.

## Practice

Use the experiment to investigate these questions:

1. What happens when you set a learning rate that is too large?
2. Can you find a starting point with a zero gradient?
3. Does a zero gradient always mean you've found a minimum?

Try the **Double well** preset. Observe how the starting point can determine which local minimum you approach.

## Summary

Gradient Descent moves **against the local gradient**. The learning rate determines the size of each move, while the shape of the function and the starting point influence where you end up.

The key idea: *the algorithm doesn't know the entire landscape—it responds to the slope where it is now.*
